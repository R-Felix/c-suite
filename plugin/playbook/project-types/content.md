# Project type: content and campaigns

Marketing campaigns, prospecting sequences, websites' copy, newsletters, guides and documentation, presentations,
social media, training material.

## Definition of done (copied into `ops/definition-of-done.md`, then adapted)

A piece is done when:
1. It serves the audience and the goal stated in the charter (one reader in mind, one action asked).
2. It follows the voice guide (tone, vocabulary, forms of address, language variant) and the brand rules.
3. Every fact, figure, name, price and promise in it was checked against a source; nothing is invented.
4. It passes the anti-generic test: it could not be sent unchanged by a competitor; it contains at least one specific
   detail about the reader or the subject.
5. Its format is final (length, channel limits, links tested, images with alt text, legal mentions where required).
6. The principal approved it for publication. **Publishing or sending is always the principal's call.**

## Typical milestones

Audience, goal and voice guide → message and outline (one sample piece approved by the principal before volume) →
production in batches → final checks → publication by the principal → measurement against the success measures.

The approved sample is the reference for every later batch: it goes in the agent frame.

## Cards and roles

| Card | Role |
|---|---|
| Research the audience or a list of recipients; collect facts about each | scout |
| Write a batch of pieces from the approved sample and voice guide | builder |
| Anything sent to real people in volume; claims with legal weight (health, money, guarantees); personal data | specialist + cold review |
| Define the message and the voice guide; a sample piece the principal will judge | architect |

Batches stay small (for example 5 to 10 pieces per card), so a returned batch costs little.

## Proof

The voice guide checklist applied piece by piece; each checked fact with its source; links opened; a sample read by
the principal before volume.

## Project-specific one-way doors

Publishing; sending to real recipients; paid promotion; using someone's name, image or testimonial; any claim that
could create a legal obligation; contacting people who did not consent where consent is required.

## Cold review checklist (in order of importance)

1. A fact, name, figure or promise that is false or unsourced; a recipient who should be excluded.
2. A legal problem: missing consent or unsubscribe, a regulated claim, a missing mention.
3. Off-voice or generic text (the anti-generic test).
4. Wrong format for the channel; broken links; missing alt text.
5. Typos and consistency (names, capitalisation, numbers).
