# Project type: research and analysis

Market studies, competitive analyses, due diligence, literature reviews, data analyses, grant or funding research,
any project whose deliverable is a finding and a recommendation.

## Definition of done (copied into `ops/definition-of-done.md`, then adapted)

A deliverable is done when:
1. It answers the question asked in the charter, and says plainly what it could not answer.
2. Every factual claim has a source the reader can open (link, document and page, dataset and query), and the
   sources are of the quality the charter requires (primary over secondary; dated; independent).
3. Facts, estimates and opinions are visibly separated; every estimate shows its method and its range.
4. Numbers were checked by a second pass (recomputed, or cross-checked against an independent source).
5. The recommendation follows from the findings and states what would change it.

## Typical milestones

Question and method (what exactly is asked, how it will be answered, what sources count) → collection → analysis →
synthesis and recommendation → review by the principal.

## Cards and roles

| Card | Role |
|---|---|
| Collect sources on a narrow question; extract facts into a table with citations | scout |
| Compare, compute, write a section from collected facts | builder |
| Figures that drive a money decision; legal or regulatory interpretation; personal data | specialist + cold review |
| Frame the question and method; resolve contradictory sources | architect |

Split collection by sub-question so that each scout returns a small table, not a pile of pages.

## Proof

The citation itself (opened, not remembered); a recomputation; a second independent source for each key number. A
claim without a source is labelled as inferred.

## Project-specific one-way doors

Contacting people (interviews, surveys, emails); paying for data or reports; sharing the findings outside the
principal's organisation; using confidential or personal data.

## Cold review checklist (in order of importance)

1. A key claim without a source, or with a source that does not say it.
2. A number wrong, double-counted, outdated or out of scope (wrong year, region, unit).
3. An estimate presented as a fact; a missing range; a method that does not support the conclusion.
4. Bias: one-sided sources, ignored contrary evidence.
5. Clarity: can the principal act on the recommendation in three minutes?
